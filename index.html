Built for formal published text

Marito is made for learning materials, examinations, curriculum documents, notices, forms and public information. It follows the Departmental standard for spelling and curriculum terminology, using the Marito Dictionaries.

Layout stays put

Send text or Markdown, from one sentence to a whole document. Layout, numbering and figures are kept in place.

Your glossary wins

Terms you supply override the model's own, so a long document uses the same word every time.

Weekly updates

Corrections from users go into the model every week. You can ask for a fixed version if you need one.

Made for editors, not instead of them

Marito gets the draft most of the way. A language editor proofreads it, and what the editor changes feeds the next update.

  1. Marito Pro translates

    It reasons about the source, translates, then post-edits its own draft against the source.

  2. A language editor proofreads

    The editor checks the draft against the points listed below.

  3. Corrections flow back

    Every correction sent back goes into the next weekly update.

What a language editor should check

Formal text is the models' strength, so check informal passages more closely. These are the points the system card asks editors to look at.

  • Names that are also words, and abbreviations replaced by descriptions.
  • Terms that drift across a long document. Use a glossary.
  • The wrong sense of a word with several meanings.
  • Agreement across long sentences.
  • Paired words collapsed, such as "panic and fear" becoming "fear and fear".
  • Familiar patterns over-applied, such as "29 questions" becoming "29 pages".
  • Renumbered or shortened lists in dense memoranda.
  • English terms left in place.
  • Stray characters from another script, which are rare.
  • Register, especially in informal text.
